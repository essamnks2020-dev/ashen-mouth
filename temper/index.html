Portfolio case study

TEMPER

People don’t regret what they meant. They regret the scorch — the midnight absolute, the “you always,” the sentence that can’t be unsent. Undo-send is a bandage after the cut. Chat rewrites feel like outsourcing your spine.

TEMPER is intentional friction designed as a forge. Heat is measured on-device from lexicon, structure, and rhythm — no model, no upload. Cooling is physical: quench, anneal hot words, optional breath into the mic. The vault stores cooled plaques. Patterns read like a smith’s notes, not a guilt dashboard.

Problem

Regret-text is a universal failure mode of digital speech. Speed is the default. Existing tools either reverse time for a few seconds or replace your voice with a generic polite mask. What’s missing is a ritual pause with agency — you still write; you just don’t fire molten.

Mechanics

  • Heat map — word-level glow for attacks, absolutes, caps-shouting, phrase bombs.
  • Anneal — tap a hot word; pick a cooler alternative that keeps intent.
  • Quench — hold to dunk; heat falls; steam and hiss give sensory proof.
  • Breath — optional mic RMS as “blow on the metal.”
  • Threshold lock — copy stays locked until the plate is tempered.
  • Vault + patterns — local plaques; time-of-day and “you”-density notes.

Visual language

Charcoal bed, molten copper, quench cyan. Syne for the brand, Outfit for UI, IBM Plex Mono for the message-as-metal. Atmosphere canvas: embers, thermometer, steam. Deliberately not purple-SaaS, not cream-terracotta lifestyle, not newspaper grid.

Monetization path

Free: full forge, local vault. Temper Pro (planned): unlimited cloud sync, deeper pattern reports, shared tempering for couples/teams, browser extension that intercepts send on Gmail/Slack/iMessage drafts. Friction as a premium value prop — the opposite of every growth-hack messenger.
