# TEMPER — portfolio case study

## One-liner

A local-first message forge that makes you cool heated text *before* you send it — through quench, anneal, and breath — instead of outsourcing your voice to a generic AI rewrite.

## The human problem

Regret texts are cheap to send and expensive to live with. The failure happens in a narrow window: composition → send. Tools today either:

1. **Undo after the fact** (Gmail’s five-second parachute), or
2. **Replace your voice** (“make this nicer”) with bland corporate tone.

What’s missing is **embodied pause with agency**. People don’t need a lecture. They need a ritual that creates space without stealing authorship.

## Insight

Friction can be the product — if it feels good. Metallurgy gave us a metaphor with real verbs: heat, quench, anneal, temper. Those verbs map cleanly to UI moves people can *feel*.

## Solution

| Layer | Choice |
| --- | --- |
| Heat | On-device lexicon + structure (caps, bangs, you-density, phrase bombs). No model, no upload. |
| Anneal | Tap a glowing word → cooler alternatives that keep intent. |
| Quench | Hold to dunk; score falls; steam + hiss as sensory proof. |
| Breath | Optional mic RMS as “blow on the metal.” |
| Lock | Copy disabled until under threshold — intentional friction. |
| Memory | Vault of plaques + smith’s-note patterns (time-of-day, accusation skew). |

## Visual system

Charcoal void, molten copper, quench cyan. Brand in **Syne**, UI in **Outfit**, message-as-metal in **IBM Plex Mono**. Full-bleed forge canvas (embers, thermometer, steam). Explicitly avoids purple-gradient SaaS, cream/terracotta lifestyle, and broadsheet clutter.
